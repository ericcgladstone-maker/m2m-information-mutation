Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit "The Modern Telephone: Information Mutation in Machine-to-Machine Communication Networks" for consideration in *Social Networks*.

The manuscript contributes to social network diffusion theory by revisiting one of its central simplifying assumptions: that network nodes transmit social contagions without semantic transformation. Brashears and Gladstone (2016) showed that this assumption fails for human relays. The present manuscript extends that line of work to networks composed of large language model agents. Using a topology-comparative simulation across six representative M2M architectures (5,880 transmission events / 420 chains), we show that artificial relay networks are not neutral conduits. Their topology shapes the magnitude *and the type* of information mutation, including drift, cross-lineage divergence, and fabrication.

The paper is designed for *Social Networks* because its primary contribution is network-theoretic. It examines how path length, centralization, input multiplicity, synthesis, feedback, and capability configuration affect the consequences of diffusion when the relay layer is artificial. The findings extend work on transmission fidelity by showing that different network architectures generate different mutation regimes and require different audit strategies, and they speak directly to the diffusion-theoretic question of how much network structure (rather than node-level capacity) determines reach and integrity.

This manuscript has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission. The submission includes a separate title page and a fully blinded manuscript prepared for double-anonymized review, along with a Highlights file, Declarations of Competing Interests and Generative AI use, a CRediT author statement, and a Data Availability Statement. Data, code, prompts, and analysis scripts will be deposited in a public repository upon publication.
